# 41. Legal texts

Part of [Qutai: how it works and the decisions behind it](../../DESIGN.md) · [All decisions](decisions-index.md) · [History](history.md)

The privacy policy and terms of use shown in the app (one set for the open-source build, another for pro-mode builds), when users must accept them again, and the open-source licences screen.

## How it works

1. There are two documents, a Privacy Policy and Terms of Use, in all ten interface languages. English and Turkish are the originals. German, Spanish, Brazilian Portuguese, French, Japanese, Korean, Russian and Indonesian are machine translations from English (20 Nov 2026, not yet reviewed by native speakers or a lawyer) in `src/legal/translations/`, loaded only when that language is shown; each ends its "Changes" section with a sentence saying the English version prevails if the translation differs. No language shows English with a note any more.
2. Each build type has its own pair. The open-source build uses `src/legal/documents.ts`, which says Qutai has no servers of its own. Pro-mode builds (with accounts and Qutai Credits) use `src/legal/proDocuments.ts`, which describes the account, credits, purchases, server backups, who processes data, how long it is kept, and who is responsible, with a contact address.
3. Each pair has a version number. Today the open-source version is **5** and the pro version is **7**, both dated 21 Nov 2026.
4. The first time Qutai is set up, the "Privacy and terms" step must be accepted. Qutai stores the version that was accepted.
5. On each launch, if the accepted version is lower than the current one, Qutai shows only the legal step, titled "We updated our privacy policy and terms". The browser stays closed until the user accepts.
6. A change to the pro texts never asks users of the open-source build again, and a change to the open-source texts never asks pro users again.
7. Ad-blocking filter lists are downloaded only after the user has accepted the terms at least once.
8. The terms of both builds (21 Nov 2026, `LEGAL-22`, `LEGAL-23`) say: Qutai is a browser and doesn't control or endorse sites; the user is responsible for the actions they ask for or approve, Qutai is not a party to their dealings with sites and sellers, and paying is off by default and needs Qutai's own dialog; AI output can be wrong and is not professional advice; blocking (ads, trackers, cookie banners, video ads) is the user's choice, uses third-party filter lists under their own licences and comes with no guarantee; styles and scripts run only in pages the user opens, after approval, and code the user writes, edits or imports is not reviewed; apps are AI-written with no warranty and imported apps are not reviewed; downloads and installs (APKs on Android) are the user's responsibility; an own key is the user's agreement with that provider; one acceptable-use list; "as is" with the liability carve-outs the law requires; and nothing limits mandatory consumer rights. The privacy policies have a section on what blocking, styles, scripts and apps do with data (`LEGAL-24`). They came in with open source 5 / pro 7 (`LEGAL-25`).
9. Each text names only the store and services of the phone it is shown on (`LEGAL-28`): on iOS the App Store, Apple's address and speech services and the Files app, never Google Play, Android or APKs; on Android Google Play, Google's services and the Downloads folder, never the App Store or the iPhone. Sign in with Apple and with Google, and Google as an AI provider or search engine, stay on both. The website's copy names both.
10. Settings › About opens the full texts with their "Updated" date, and Settings › About › Open source licences lists every third-party component in the app (134 entries) with its version, licence and platform. The list is searchable, and tapping an entry shows the full licence text.
11. Version history:
   - Open source: 1 on 14 Nov, 2 on 16 Nov (what actually leaves the phone, and what the app enforces about passwords, cards, IBANs and ID numbers), 3 on 19 Nov (contacts, page styles, the cookie list), 4 on 20 Nov (option-card pictures loaded from sites, the Turkish spelling of suffixes on the name, the eight translations, the contacts example in each language's own name), 5 on 21 Nov (protective clauses in the terms; blocking, styles, scripts and apps in the privacy policy).
   - Pro: 2 on 16 Nov (its own texts), 3 on 17 Nov (server backups), 4 on 17 Nov (who is responsible, contact, sign-in by email, purchases, processors, IP addresses and logs, retention, security), 5 on 19 Nov (contacts), 6 on 20 Nov (as open source 4, and "sign-in tokens" spelled out where the server's logs are described), 7 on 21 Nov (as open source 5, the reports section, the contact address, and credits that do not expire).

**Licences screen**

- (was `LEGAL-16`) The licence script warns about a package without a licence file, a copyleft licence or one it doesn't recognise. Nothing copyleft-only ships: FreeType is used under its FTL licence and Zstandard under BSD. Where: README.md › License; 2275af2.

## Limits and what is not built

- The blocking clause says the blocking features are "features you choose" and can each be turned off; it does not say they are off by default, because "Block ads and trackers", "Hide cookie banners" and "Block third-party cookies" are on by default (`BLK-1`, `BLK-19`, `BLK-20`); only "Block video ads" is off until turned on (`BLK-29`).
- The sections added on 21 Nov 2026 were machine-translated into the eight languages the same day (`LEGAL-12`).
- The eight translations are machine translations, not reviewed by native speakers or a lawyer (LEGAL-12).
- (planned) Lawyer review, store privacy labels and the Google Play Data safety form.
- The pro privacy policy's "Purchases in the app" says RevenueCat receives the account number and the store's purchase record, but not that its SDK also gets the IP address and basic device and app information, nor the price and recent-purchase lookups (`BUY-29`, 20 Nov 2026). Proposed sentence for the owner, after "…restored on another phone.": "When you open Buy credits, and at each start of the app on a phone where you have opened it before, the app also asks RevenueCat for the packs' prices and your recent purchases; with these requests RevenueCat receives your IP address and basic information about the phone and the app (system, app version, store country and language)." Not edited: it needs another pro legal bump, the owner's call. The same section's "restored on another phone" means the credits in the account, not a store restore.
- Licences: Gradle's transitive libraries are not listed (no Android SDK on the machine that generated the list), two Expo Android libraries' licences are unconfirmed, and the screen has not been tried on a phone.
